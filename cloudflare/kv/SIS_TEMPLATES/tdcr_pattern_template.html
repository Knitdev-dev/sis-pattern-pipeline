Round-Neck Raglan Sweater

1. Pattern Details

About This Pattern

This sweater is worked seamlessly from the top down in stockinette stitch. You begin at the round neckline with a folded rib collar, then shape the yoke with raglan increases — using short rows along the way to lift the back neck. Once the yoke is the right depth, the sleeve stitches are placed on hold and the body is worked in the round to its full length, finishing with a ribbed band at the hem. Each sleeve is then picked up at the underarm and worked in the round down to a ribbed cuff. The sweater has no seams.

A Note on Gauge

Getting the fit right depends on gauge — and gauge can be surprisingly unpredictable. Even if your swatch matched, the fabric of a finished sweater often behaves differently: gravity, fabric weight, and the sheer volume of the piece can all shift things slightly. Ideally, gauge is measured over a larger area on a garment that has already been knitted, blocked, and worn. That's not always practical, of course — but it's worth keeping in mind.

This pattern uses half-twisted rib (k1 tbl, p1) at the collar, cuffs, and hem — estimated at [rib_gauge_sts] sts per 10cm against your [Gauge_sts] sts stockinette gauge. It knits up a little tighter than standard 1×1 rib, but gives a neater, more defined edge.

Stitch Library — German short rows technique: tutorial.
Notes on German short rows: Each time you work the German short rows, turn the work, slip the first stitch purlwise with the yarn in front, then pull the yarn firmly up and over the needle so you see two "legs" of the stitch. This is called the double stitch — although it is really just one stitch. On a following row, when you reach a double stitch made earlier, work its two legs together as a single stitch (knit them together on RS rows, purl them together on WS rows). This closes the gaps and is what makes the short rows invisible. It is all shown in the tutorial above.

5. Sleeves

Make 2 identical sleeves

Both sleeves are worked identically. Work the first sleeve complete before beginning the second.

Locate the cast-on [UA_pickup] sts on the body's underarm. Hold your work with the top up and the body's underarm cast-on stitches in the middle. Place the BOR marker on your right needle, then, starting from the middle of the underarm cast-on stitches, pick up and knit half of them. Place a new marker, then pick up and knit one extra stitch in the corner between the cast-on stitches and the sleeve's stitches on the holder (see the video below). This helps to avoid a gap that would otherwise form in the corner.

On picking up in the sleeve's corners: Refer to this video for tips on how to do this. Note that the sweater in the video is knitted from the hem up, while ours is knitted top-down — this means the underarm stitches you pick up should be the upside-down V's, as opposed to the video.

Now you have half of the underarm stitches plus the extra corner stitch on your right needle.

Place the [Y4_slv] sts from the stitch holder on your working left needle, starting from right to left, knit them to one stitch before the end of held stitches. Place another marker, k1.

Pick up and knit an extra stitch in the sleeve's second corner as you did for the first one. Pick up and knit the remaining half of the underarm cast-on stitches. You are now back at the BOR marker.

There are now [SL_sts] sts on the needle, plus the extra stitch in each corner. These will be decreased in the next round.

On the next round, knit to the first corner marker, remove it, k2tog (the extra stitch together with the next stitch), knit to the second corner marker, remove it, ssk (the last sleeve stitch together with the extra stitch), knit to the end of round.
